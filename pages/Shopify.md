# History
collapsed:: true
	- **How**: Shopify was founded in **2006** by **Tobias Lütke**, **Daniel Weinand**, and **Scott Lake** as an e-commerce platform designed to help merchants build their own online stores. The founders originally wanted to sell snowboards online, but created the platform when they couldn’t find a suitable e-commerce solution.
	- **Who**: Shopify was created by the **Shopify Inc.** team, with **Tobias Lütke** as the CEO. The company quickly grew, becoming one of the largest e-commerce platforms worldwide, used by businesses of all sizes to set up and manage online stores.
	- **Why**: Shopify was created to provide an easy-to-use, flexible, and scalable e-commerce platform for merchants, enabling them to sell products online with minimal technical expertise.
- # Introduction
  collapsed:: true
	- ## Advantages:
		- **Ease of Use**: Shopify is known for its user-friendly interface, making it easy for non-technical users to set up and manage an online store with little effort.
		- **Scalability**: Shopify supports businesses of all sizes, from small startups to large enterprises, with features designed to grow as the business expands.
		- **Customizable Themes and Apps**: Shopify offers a large selection of customizable themes and a wide range of apps to add advanced features like payment gateways, inventory management, and marketing tools.
		- **Integrated Payment System**: Shopify includes a built-in payment gateway (Shopify Payments), simplifying the payment process and offering competitive transaction fees.
		- **24/7 Support**: Shopify provides excellent customer support, with access to a help center, live chat, and email support, as well as a community forum for troubleshooting.
		- **Mobile Optimization**: Shopify’s themes are mobile-responsive, ensuring that online stores are optimized for smartphones and tablets.
	-
	- ## **Disadvantages** :
		- **Monthly Fees**: Shopify operates on a subscription model, which may be costly for smaller businesses or those with tight budgets. Additional fees may apply for third-party payment gateways and apps.
		- **Limited Customization**: While Shopify is highly customizable, it can be restrictive compared to open-source platforms like **WooCommerce** or **Magento**, particularly for users looking for very specific or unique features.
		- **Transaction Fees**: Shopify charges additional transaction fees if you don’t use its built-in payment gateway (Shopify Payments), which can increase costs for businesses using external payment processors.
		- **Learning Curve for Advanced Features**: While easy to set up, more complex features (like customizations or using apps) can require a learning curve or technical expertise.
	-
	- ## Remember Points :
		- **User-Friendly Interface**: Shopify is designed for users with minimal technical experience, offering a simple and intuitive platform for building online stores.
		- **Scalable and Flexible**: Shopify works for businesses of all sizes, with tools that support growth and expansion.
		- **Integrated Payment and Support**: Includes built-in payment processing and excellent customer support available around the clock.
		- **Ongoing Costs**: Subscription fees, transaction fees, and additional app costs can add up, so businesses should plan accordingly.
		- **Great for E-Commerce**: Ideal for businesses focused solely on e-commerce, though it may not offer the same flexibility as open-source platforms for custom projects.
