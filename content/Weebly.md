- # History
	- **How**: Weebly was founded in **2006** by **David Rusenko**, **Chris Fanini**, and **Dan Veltri** as a website builder aimed at helping individuals and small businesses create websites without needing coding skills.
	-
	- **Who**: Developed by **Weebly, Inc.**, which was acquired by **Square** (now **Block, Inc.**) in **2018**. Weebly provides an easy-to-use platform primarily for building small business websites, blogs, and e-commerce stores.
	-
	- **Why**: Weebly was created to empower users with no technical background to build professional-looking websites, offering an intuitive drag-and-drop interface and hosting solutions.
-
- # Introduction
	- ## Advantages:
		- **Ease of Use**: Weebly offers a drag-and-drop builder, making it very user-friendly for beginners and those with little technical expertise.
		- **Affordable Pricing**: Weebly offers competitive pricing plans, including a free plan with essential features, making it accessible for small businesses and personal projects.
		- **Built-in E-Commerce Features**: Weebly provides a robust e-commerce platform, allowing users to set up online stores, manage inventory, track orders, and accept payments.
		- **Responsive Design**: All themes provided by Weebly are mobile-friendly, ensuring that websites work seamlessly across devices.
		- **Integrated Tools**: Weebly offers built-in marketing tools such as SEO features, email marketing, and analytics, making it easier to grow your website and reach customers.
		- **Free Plan Available**: Weebly offers a free plan that includes basic features, ideal for users who want to build a simple site with a Weebly subdomain.
	-
	- ## Disadvantages:
		- **Limited Customization**: Weebly’s design options are more limited compared to other website builders like **Wix** or **Squarespace**, making it less flexible for users who want more control over the look and feel of their site.
		- **Fewer Features for Large E-Commerce Sites**: While Weebly’s e-commerce features are solid for small stores, it may lack advanced tools required for large-scale or high-volume e-commerce businesses.
		- **Template Lock-In**: Once a template is selected, switching to another template requires rebuilding the site, which can be inconvenient if you want to change the design later.
		- **Limited App Integrations**: Weebly offers fewer third-party apps and integrations compared to other platforms like **Shopify** or **Wix**, which can restrict the addition of advanced features.
		- **SEO Limitations**: While Weebly offers some SEO tools, it’s generally considered less flexible than other website builders when it comes to advanced SEO optimization.
		-
	- ## Remember Points:
		- **User-Friendly and Affordable**: Weebly is ideal for small businesses, personal websites, or simple e-commerce stores, with an easy-to-use interface and budget-friendly pricing.
		- **Limited Design Flexibility**: It’s less customizable compared to other website builders like **Wix** or **Squarespace**, which may be a drawback for users who want more design freedom.
		- **Good for Small to Medium-Sized Stores**: Weebly provides solid e-commerce features for small businesses but may not be suitable for large-scale or complex e-commerce sites.
		- **Free Plan Available**: Offers a free plan with essential features, though a premium plan is required for custom domains, more storage, and advanced tools.
		- **Built-In Marketing Tools**: Weebly’s built-in SEO tools, email marketing, and analytics make it a convenient platform for users looking to promote their website and track performance.
